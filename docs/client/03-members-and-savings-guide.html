FinanceFlo Members & Savings

The basics

Customers and members

FinFlo keeps a simple distinction that matches how your institution actually works. A customer is the person on your books — the verified identity that your loans, savings accounts, and records are attached to. When you give that person access to their own branded app, they become a member: the same person, now with a self-service login to your portal.

You always see one consistent record. Everything a customer does in their app — completing their profile, uploading documents, setting up savings — flows straight back into the same record your team reviews, so the business side and the customer side never drift apart.

Customer — the record

The verified identity on your books: personal details, identity documents, accounts, and history. This is what your team works with day to day.

Member — the login

The same person, given access to your branded app so they can view balances, repay loans, manage savings, and move money themselves.

Getting started

Inviting and onboarding a customer

Bringing a customer on board starts with a single secure invitation. You send them a private invite link from your dashboard; that link is tied to their email, so only the person you intended can claim the account. When they open it, they're guided straight into a short, friendly sign-up — no paperwork to chase, no forms to mail back.

You send the invite

From your dashboard, send the customer a private invitation link to join your branded app.

Locked to the right person

The link is tied to the invited email and can't be changed, so only the intended customer can claim the account.

They start sign-up

On accepting, the customer is taken straight into the guided sign-up, ready to complete it at their own pace.

Identity verification

Know-your-customer, made simple

FinFlo gives every customer a secure document vault. Your customers upload the identity documents you need — their CNIC, a selfie, a utility bill, and more — and your staff review each one and mark it Verified or Rejected. Documents that expire can carry an expiry date, and the platform reminds you before they lapse, so your records stay current without anyone having to remember.

Document types you can collect

CNIC Selfie Utility Bill Tax Return Proof of Residence Other

The status of each one

Pending Verified Rejected Expired
StatusWhat it means for you
PendingThe customer has uploaded the document and it's waiting in your review queue.
VerifiedYour staff have checked it and confirmed it's valid — the customer is good to proceed.
RejectedThe document failed review (unclear, wrong type, or doesn't match) and needs to be re-submitted.
ExpiredThe document has passed its expiry date; you'll have been reminded ahead of time to refresh it.
Verify before you activate

Because identity documents carry a clear status, your team can confirm exactly who a customer is before opening accounts or lending — and the whole vault is kept private to your business.

Accounts

Accounts your members can hold

Each member can hold several kinds of account, each kept separate so balances are never mixed up. Savings and shares sit apart from everyday spending money, and a member's own account numbers are self-describing at a glance.

Current account

The everyday wallet for deposits, withdrawals, and loan proceeds.

Savings account

Held apart from everyday money and earns profit on a schedule.

Term deposit

A fixed-term deposit with a maturity date, tracked to payout.

AccountNumber starts withWhat it holds

Savings that grow

Savings that earn, automatically

When your members save with you, their money works for them. FinFlo calculates each saver's profit and shares it into their savings balance on a regular schedule — you don't have to run the numbers by hand or remember to pay it out. Each account can carry its own profit rate, so different savings products can reward members differently.

Profit, calculated for you

The platform works out each saver's profit automatically, based on the rate set for their account.

Shared on schedule

Earned profit is moved into the member's savings balance on a regular cadence, with no manual step.

Members' money, kept theirs

Shares a member places with you are treated as the member's own money — never as your income — and are kept separate from their everyday wallet.

Built for committees and cooperatives

If your institution runs share capital or committee-style savings, member shares are always accounted for as money you owe back to the member, kept apart from their loans, and never touched to cover a repayment without the member's instruction.

Moving money

Transfers, protected and limited

Members can move money the way they expect to. They can shift funds between their own accounts, send to other people, and pay out over Raast and the wider banking system. Every outgoing transfer is protected by the member's private security PIN, and sensible daily limits keep any single account from sending more than it should.

Between their own accounts

Members move money between their current and savings accounts in a couple of taps.

Send to others & out to banks

Send money to other people, including over Raast and bank rails to outside accounts.

PIN-protected

Each withdrawal or transfer asks for the member's private security PIN before it goes through.

Sensible daily limits

Per-day limits cap how much can leave an account, protecting members and your business alike.
